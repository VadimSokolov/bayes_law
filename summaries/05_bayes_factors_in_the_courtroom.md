# Chapter 5. Bayes Factors in the Courtroom

This chapter asks how a Bayes factor works when the competing legal propositions are composite, that is, families of distributions with unknown parameters, as they are for DNA profiles with laboratory error, low template DNA and secondary transfer, and for statistical evidence in discrimination cases. The Bayes factor becomes a ratio of marginal likelihoods (predictive success), and the chapter works through DNA match probability plus error rate, a low template detection model, the database search controversy (cold hit, trawling, the island problem prior), p values versus Bayes factors with the Sellke, Bayarri and Berger bound, Lindley's paradox, robust Bayesian sensitivity over ranges of priors, a footwear mark example, hierarchical and beta predictive models for small forensic databases, and the Balding and Nichols subpopulation formula. It also treats composite theories as mixtures over subplots, the Bayesian Occam factor and The Popi M, and it ends with principles for communicating Bayes factors to a fact finder. It shows that laboratory error can cap the weight of a DNA match, and that the two sides of the database debate answer different questions.
