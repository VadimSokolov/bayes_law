This appendix is a glossary of the legal, statistical and decision theoretic terms used in the book, in alphabetical order, each with a one or two sentence definition. It runs from the absolute priority rule in cramdowns under Chapter 11 of the US Bankruptcy Code and activity level propositions in forensic science, through the adversarial procedure, balance of probabilities, base rate and Bayes factor, to the later terms of the alphabet. It serves lawyers who meet an unfamiliar statistical term and statisticians who meet an unfamiliar legal one. Statistical entries include the likelihood ratio, the probability of evidence under one hypothesis divided by its probability under another, decibans, ten times the base ten logarithm of the likelihood ratio, and the Kelly criterion for sizing a sequence of independent bets. Legal entries include the but for test, loss of a chance, fraud on the market, plausibility pleading and intime conviction, the inner conviction of the judge or jury that is the standard of French criminal procedure.

## From the literature

The glossary defines terms drawn from the standard literature, including the Bayes factor, base rate, balance of probabilities, activity level proposition and the absolute priority rule, using the definitions as the chapters use them.
