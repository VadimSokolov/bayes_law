This appendix gives a sample three hour examination for a law school course built on the book, with notes on what a strong answer contains. The questions are original and the facts invented. Question 1 concerns a courier charged with importing drugs, and asks students to state the propositions, estimate likelihood ratios for four items of evidence, identify items that are not conditionally independent, compute a posterior and draft a jury direction on nervousness. Question 2 concerns a delayed diagnosis of a heart attack and asks for advice under English law with reference to Barnett, Hotson and Gregg v Scott, the probability of causation computed from risks of sixty and thirty five per cent, the French approach of loss of a chance, and proportional recovery. Question 3 concerns a machine learning tool predicting failure to appear, and asks about calibration, group fairness, the selective labels problem and the implied loss ratio of a twenty per cent threshold. Students use it to practise and instructors to set papers.

## From the literature

The questions draw on Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington HMC [1969], Hotson v East Berkshire HA [1987], Gregg v Scott [2005], the but for test and loss of a chance, the selective labels problem and calibration of risk assessment tools.
