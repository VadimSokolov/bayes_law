This chapter treats antitrust as decision making under uncertainty about future harm. It begins with predicting harm and with error costs (false positives and false negatives) in enforcement, and discusses the debate over error cost analysis. It then covers cartel detection, where statistical screens are read as likelihood ratios, with the LIBOR manipulation episode as an example; damages, including the overcharge, class certification, and pass on; and merger review, including the prediction problem, market definition and the hypothetical monopolist test, upward pricing pressure, merger simulation with Bayesian demand estimation (random coefficient logit models), and merger retrospectives. A final section considers dominance in digital markets. Cases include Illinois Brick, Hanover Shoe and Comcast Corp v Behrend. Readers looking for the Bayesian treatment of collusion screens, damages models in class actions, direct purchaser and indirect purchaser rules, the Berry Levinsohn Pakes demand model, or merger simulation will find the chapter's argument and sources here.

## From the literature

Error costs and the limits of antitrust (Easterbrook 1984; Baker 2015); variance screens for collusion (Abrantes Metz, Froeb, Geweke and Taylor 2006); LIBOR manipulation screens (Abrantes Metz, Kraten, Metz and Seow 2012); Hanover Shoe and Illinois Brick on pass on and standing; Comcast Corp v Behrend, 569 U.S. 27 (2013), on damages models and class certification; the hypothetical monopolist test and an alternative to market definition (Farrell and Shapiro 2010); automobile demand estimation (Berry, Levinsohn and Pakes 1995); Bayesian random coefficient logit models with aggregate data (Jiang, Manchanda and Rossi 2009). The chapter also cites the authors' own work on Bayesian BLP estimation (Polson and Sokolov).
