How does the Bayesian pleadings analysis work on concrete claims, and what step does it indicate? The chapter gives a six step recipe (list the elements, map the pleadings, assign issue probabilities, handle dependence, compute the probability of success and expected recovery, compare the expected value of continuing with the alternatives) and applies it to five illustrative cases. Product negligence, after Donoghue v Stevenson, combines duty, breach and causation with positive correlation and ends in settlement. An exclusion clause, after Photo Production v Securicor, shows that uncertainty about the law must be priced and makes an appeal on a point of law the indicated step. A negligent misstatement by an investment adviser, after Hedley Byrne and Manchester Building Society v Grant Thornton, multiplies falsity, breach and scope of duty and points to mediation. Age discrimination pleading, after Swierkiewicz v Sorema, Twombly and Iqbal, multiplies likelihood ratios for the replacement, the reviews and the remarks. A price fixing complaint after Twombly shows how parallel conduct, the lack of a cost explanation and an industry meeting move the posterior, with the outcome depending on the plausibility threshold and on plus factors. A table collects the decisions, and the chapter ends by explaining how a network could be trained on such labelled cases. The numbers are illustrative.

## From the literature

Donoghue v Stevenson (1932), Photo Production v Securicor (1980), Hedley Byrne (1964), Manchester Building Society v Grant Thornton (2021), Swierkiewicz v Sorema (2002), Bell Atlantic v Twombly (2007), Ashcroft v Iqbal (2009), McDonnell Douglas, res ipsa loquitur and plus factors in conspiracy pleading, section 2(1) of the Misrepresentation Act 1967, and Gordon's Pleadings Game (1995). The worked probabilities, the table of decisions and the six step recipe are presented by the chapter as the authors' illustrative analysis, with numbers chosen to be defensible on the reported facts and not to second guess the courts. The training proposal refers to Polson and Sokolov's Pleadings Game paper.
