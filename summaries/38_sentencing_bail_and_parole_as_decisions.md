# Chapter 38. Sentencing, Bail and Parole as Decisions

This chapter treats sentencing, bail and parole as decisions under uncertainty that follow the verdict. It sets out the objectives of sentencing (deterrence, incapacitation, retribution and rehabilitation), guidelines as decision rules (the Sentencing Council guidelines in England and Wales and the federal guidelines after Booker), and bail, framed as a decision with Type I and Type II errors, together with reform. It then covers parole and recall, the cost of custody, evidence on what works (randomised and quasi experimental studies of incarceration, probation and related programmes), and machines in sentencing, including machine predictions of risk. Cases include United States v Booker and Secretary of State for the Home Department v Sim. The Independent Sentencing Review chaired by David Gauke (2025) is discussed. Readers searching for risk assessment, pretrial detention, algorithmic bail decisions, sentencing guidelines, recidivism prediction, parole boards, or the cost of imprisonment will find the chapter's argument and its sources here.

## From the literature

United States v Booker, 543 U.S. 220 (2005), on advisory guidelines; Secretary of State for the Home Department v Sim [2003] EWCA Civ 1845, on the parole release test; machine predictions of bail decisions (Kleinberg, Lakkaraju, Leskovec, Ludwig and Mullainathan 2018); the Independent Sentencing Review: Final Report (Gauke 2025); the Sentencing Council guidelines for England and Wales.
