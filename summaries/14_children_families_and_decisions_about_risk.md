How do family courts decide under uncertainty about past events and future risk to children? The chapter sets out the section 31 Children Act 1989 threshold criteria as two thresholds: past facts proved on the balance of probabilities, and future harm established as a real possibility. It treats uncertain perpetrators as a pool of alternatives with posterior probabilities (Re H (Minors), Lancashire County Council v B, Re S-B), the reliability of expert evidence of inflicted injury, and proportionality in adoption (Re B (Care Proceedings: Appeal), Re B-S (Children)) as a call for holistic decision analysis across realistic options. It also considers the welfare principle and the presumption of parental involvement as a prior, and predictive risk models in child protection (the Allegheny County model, calibration, disparate impact, selective labels). Exercises compute pool membership from posteriors and a likelihood ratio for a skeletal finding.
