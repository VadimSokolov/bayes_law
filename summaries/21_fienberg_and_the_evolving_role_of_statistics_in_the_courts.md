What did Stephen Fienberg show about the use of statistics in courts and public decisions? The chapter surveys his work: the 1989 National Research Council report The Evolving Role of Statistical Assessments as Evidence in the Courts and its six findings; a model of the adversarial expert in which each party calls the most favourable of k experts, so the court sees order statistics that exaggerate real uncertainty, with the English remedies of the duty to the court, the single joint expert and concurrent evidence under the Civil Procedure Rules; and the misreading of significance tests. Castaneda v Partida (1977) and the two or three standard deviation rule are analysed with a Bayes factor and Lindley's paradox, together with Hazelwood and the choice of the relevant population, and Duren v Missouri on the fair cross section, absolute and comparative disparity. The chapter also covers his analysis of the 1969 draft lottery, the census adjustment dispute (Anderson and Fienberg, Department of Commerce v House of Representatives), the 2003 National Research Council report on the polygraph with its base rate argument for screening, and Fienberg and Kadane's 1983 advice that experts present likelihoods or Bayes factors and a range of priors, not a posterior probability of guilt.

## From the literature

The draft lottery study (Fienberg 1971). The report of the Panel on Statistical Assessments as Evidence in the Courts (Fienberg 1989). The polygraph report (National Research Council 2003). Census politics (Anderson and Fienberg 1999). Presentation of Bayesian analyses in court (Fienberg and Kadane 1983). Castaneda v Partida (US Supreme Court 1977). Hazelwood School District v United States (1977). Duren v Missouri (1979). Department of Commerce v United States House of Representatives (1999). Federal Rule of Evidence 706, and CPR 35.3, 35.7 and Practice Direction 35. Jury selection analysis (Kadane 2008). Finkelstein and Levin (2015). The order statistics model of adversarial experts and the Bayesian reading of Castaneda are the chapter's own worked analysis.
