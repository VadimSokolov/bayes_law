What does the collapse of Greensill Capital show about reliance, representations and information? The chapter uses supply chain finance (reverse factoring) as a case study, with the Credit Suisse funds, trade credit insurance, GFG Alliance, SoftBank and Katerra, and relies on public statements, official reports and judgments rather than contested allegations. It models the chain of beliefs from obligors to investors as a sequence of channels where information is lost, concentration and tail risk in a simulated loss distribution, future receivables as a different hypothesis from existing receivables, Beta posterior updating on late and rolled over payments, and a Bayesian network showing that insurance written on the originator's presentation is not an independent signal. It asks the insurance questions under fair presentation, discusses directors' disqualification and lobbying as value of information, and measures loss and causation. It draws four lessons: chains weaken information, tails matter, dependent signals should not be added, and remedies should be counterfactual and proportional.
