# Chapter 32. Arbitration, Mediation and Private Adjudication

From *Bayes and Law: Evidence, Expected Utility and Machines in the Courtroom* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 32. This chapter applies the book's decision framework to private adjudication and to the choice of forum. It explains why commercial parties arbitrate (neutrality, expertise, confidentiality, finality, enforceability under the New York Convention 1958) in terms of accuracy, cost of delay and the value of appeals. It treats the selection of party appointed arbitrators as a game and suggests a Bayesian shrinkage model of arbitrator tendencies. It covers consumer and employment arbitration and class action waivers under the Federal Arbitration Act (AT&T Mobility v Concepcion, Epic Systems v Lewis), mediation and the zone of agreement in the settlement model, English court encouragement of mediation (Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil 2023), investment treaty arbitration and proposals for an appellate mechanism or investment court, and online dispute resolution including the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal. It argues that machine assisted adjudication needs transparency and contestability.

## From the literature

AT&T Mobility LLC v Concepcion (2011); Epic Systems Corp v Lewis (2018); Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil County Borough Council [2023] EWCA Civ 1416; the New York Convention of 1958; the Federal Arbitration Act. The settlement condition for mediation and the small claims argument rest on models developed in earlier chapters of the book, and the chapter applies them to forum choice.
