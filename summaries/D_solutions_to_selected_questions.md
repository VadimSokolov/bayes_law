This appendix gives worked answers to the computational questions at the ends of the chapters, and brief guidance on some of the discussion questions, organised chapter by chapter. Numbers are rounded and for some questions only part of the question is answered. The answers use the tools of the book: decision thresholds and the ratio of losses they imply, binomial calculations such as the probability that a jury of twelve reaches a correct verdict, posterior odds from likelihood ratios, the island formula, and Bayes factors. The first solutions show, for example, that a threshold of 0.95 implies a loss ratio of 19 and a threshold of one half implies equal losses. The third shows that, with a uniform prior over one million people and a feature shared by one in a thousand, the posterior that a matching suspect is the offender is about 0.001. A student uses the appendix to check an answer after attempting the question, and is advised to verify the figures with the code of Appendix B.

## From the literature

The solutions apply standard results presented in the chapters: decision thresholds from expected loss, the binomial model of jury accuracy, Bayes' rule in odds form, and the island formula of Balding and Donnelly (1995).
