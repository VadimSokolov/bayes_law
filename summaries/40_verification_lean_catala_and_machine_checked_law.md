Can formal verification check the logical and arithmetical steps of legal decisions? The chapter treats a judgment as an argument from findings of fact and law to an order, and asks which steps a proof assistant such as Lean can certify. It introduces Lean and its kernel, writes the Hand formula in the Carroll Towing example and a conjunction of the elements of a negligence claim as short theorems, and reviews rules as code: the Prolog encoding of the British Nationality Act 1981, Lawsky's default logic for tax statutes, and the Catala language. It then verifies Bayes factor computations such as the island problem, with interval certificates for ranges of a contamination rate, and treats defeasibility and burden of proof. It argues that facts, open texture and discretion with values cannot be verified, and that autoformalisation errors become hidden premises, citing the Dutch childcare benefits scandal and the Post Office Horizon cases. It ends with three proposals: publish statutory computations as code, attach checkable specifications to forensic likelihood ratios, and use structural checks on trial findings on appeal.
