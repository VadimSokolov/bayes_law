This appendix is a short self contained primer on the probability the book uses, written for readers with no statistics. It states the three rules of probability, Bayes' rule in probability and odds form, and independence and conditional independence. It then covers evidence: the likelihood ratio and weight of evidence, composite hypotheses, the island formula for a match when the source may be someone else in a population, uncertain match probabilities, and beta binomial prediction. A section on decisions gives expected utility, the decision threshold that follows from the losses of the two kinds of error, and the value of information. Short sections on the binomial, Poisson, beta, gamma and normal distributions, a list of named fallacies (prosecutor's fallacy, defence fallacy, Dershowitz fallacy, independence fallacy, base rate neglect, double counting, hindsight bias, selection neglect) and a note on graphical models and Bayesian networks, including blocking and colliders, close it. A reader uses it as a reference to look up a rule or definition when a chapter relies on it.

## From the literature

Bayes' rule and odds form; likelihood ratio as weight of evidence; the island problem and its generalisation (Balding and Donnelly 1995); the prosecutor's fallacy (Balding and Donnelly 1994); expected utility and decision thresholds; beta binomial prediction; Bayesian networks as factorisations of a joint distribution.
