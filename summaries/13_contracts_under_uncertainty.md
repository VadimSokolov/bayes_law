What does contract law look like when parties and courts act under uncertainty? The chapter treats contracts as bets and as messages. It reads interpretation as inference about what a reasonable person with the background knowledge of the parties would have understood (Investors Compensation Scheme v West Bromwich, Arnold v Britton, Wood v Capita, Marks and Spencer v BNP Paribas on implied terms), and remoteness of damage as a probability and assumed responsibility question (Hadley v Baxendale, The Heron II, The Achilleas). It then treats penalty clauses (Cavendish Square v Makdessi), frustration as allocation of unforeseen risk (Taylor v Caldwell, Krell v Henry, Davis Contractors v Fareham), the Uniform Commercial Code, and machines and contracts. The chapter argues that these doctrines allocate risks the parties did not price, and that the Bayesian reading shows when a court should fill gaps and when it should leave the allocation to the parties.
