How should courts judge solvency after a firm has failed, without hindsight? The chapter treats the balance sheet, unreasonably small capital and ability to pay tests of fraudulent transfer law as three posteriors, following Heaton's reading through financial economics. It uses the Merton structural model to turn leverage into a probability of default, a simple model of hindsight bias (Kamin and Rachlinski) in which cases reaching court have failed, and market evidence including credit default swap spreads and a precision weighted combination of market and expert estimates (VFB v Campbell Soup, In re Iridium, Moody v Security Pacific). It covers leveraged buyouts (Tribune, Merit Management v FTI), Chapter 11 feasibility and the Till cramdown rate, absolute priority and Harrington v Purdue Pharma on third party releases, Greensill revisited, and machine learning for solvency prediction, including Altman's Z score.
