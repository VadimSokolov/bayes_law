This chapter asks how base rate reasoning and the law of privacy apply to digital evidence and surveillance. It treats facial recognition as the island problem at scale: a false match rate multiplied by a database size gives the expected number of false candidates, so a match is a lead, not an identification. It discusses R (Bridges) v Chief Constable of South Wales Police [2020] on live facial recognition, the public sector equality duty and error rates by group. It covers cell site location data, Carpenter v United States (2018), what location evidence proves and its likelihood ratio, and geofence warrants as database searches. It treats keyword searches, technology assisted review and disclosure failures such as the Liam Allan case, with recall and precision as measures. It considers authentication of digital evidence, hash values, deepfakes and cryptographic provenance. It closes with privacy as a constraint on evidence, mass searches, and individualised suspicion read as a condition on the prior.

## From the literature
