What will AI change in law, what can it promise, and what can it not? The chapter predicts five changes: legal information becomes cheap, prediction becomes explicit, disputes are selected differently as better prediction raises settlement (Priest and Klein), evidence changes through deepfakes and algorithmic decisions that become evidence, and overloaded courts triage. It lists what AI can promise: consistency against noise, coherence in updating and thresholds (Lindley's challenge), access to justice, citing the California 2024 Justice Gap Study, and verification in which machines draft, verifiers check and people judge. It lists what AI cannot promise: choosing values, handling facts absent from its data, and legitimacy, which depends on accountable persons and fair process. It closes with what a lawyer of the future should know, arguing that understanding likelihood ratios and calibration will be part of competence.
