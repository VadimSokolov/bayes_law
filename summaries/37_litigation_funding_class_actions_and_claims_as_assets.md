This chapter treats legal claims as risky assets and applies decision theory to their valuation, funding and aggregation. It gives the expected value of a claim as a discounted sum over outcomes, and analyses the funder's decision for a single claim, for portfolios, and for how much to invest (a Kelly criterion argument). It covers the law of funding, including the doctrines of maintenance and champerty, England after the PACCAR decision, and funder control and conflicts. It treats class and collective actions, with Merricks v Mastercard, aggregation and aggregate damages, then claims in sovereign and commercial disputes, insurance and the pricing of legal risk, and policy. Bates v Post Office appears as an example of group litigation and funding. Readers searching for third party litigation funding, litigation finance, litigation funding agreements, damages based agreements, collective proceedings, opt out class actions, claim valuation, or portfolio theory for claims will find the chapter's argument and its sources here.

## From the literature

The Kelly criterion (Kelly 1956; MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba 2011); R (PACCAR Inc) v Competition Appeal Tribunal [2023] UKSC 28; Mastercard Inc v Merricks [2020] UKSC 51; Bates v Post Office Ltd (No 3: Common Issues) [2019] EWHC 606 (QB); Bates v Post Office Ltd (No 6: Horizon Issues) [2019] EWHC 3408 (QB). The doctrines of maintenance and champerty are described as the historical background.
