# Chapter 24. Securities Fraud, Event Studies and Prices as Evidence

This chapter asks how courts use market prices and event studies as evidence in securities fraud and what a Bayesian reading adds. It sets out the fraud on the market presumption of Basic v Levinson, price impact after Halliburton II and Goldman Sachs v Arkansas Teacher Retirement System, and loss causation under Dura Pharmaceuticals v Broudo. It explains the market model, abnormal returns, cumulative abnormal returns and the low power of single firm tests, and argues that courts misuse significance thresholds. It proposes posterior probabilities of price impact, a prior with mass at zero, heavy tailed priors such as the Cauchy and horseshoe, hierarchical models across disclosure dates, and posterior distributions for inflation and damages. It turns to the Autonomy litigation, ACL Netherlands v Lynch [2022] EWHC 1178 (Ch), section 90A of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and reliance, the differing civil and criminal outcomes, and the valuation counterfactual. It closes with prices as evidence in Delaware appraisal, antitrust overcharge and prediction markets.
