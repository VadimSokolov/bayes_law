How should a court turn uncertain evidence into a sum of money? The chapter treats an award as a number drawn from a predictive distribution and asks which summary of it the compensatory principle requires. It covers future losses and lump sums (Wells v Wells on discount rates, the Ogden tables, contingencies, and periodical payments under the Damages Act 1996, Thompstone), lost profits and the American reasonable certainty rule (Story Parchment), loss of a chance in contract (Chaplin v Hicks), and presumptions that resolve uncertainty against the wrongdoer (Armory v Delamirie), read as a choice of posterior quantile. It discusses negotiating damages (Morris-Garner v One Step, Sempra Metals), hindsight in assessment (The Golden Victory), combining expert estimates, interest and the time value of money, and the use of data in damages. The chapter argues that liability is judged on the information at the time of the act while quantum may use later information, and it returns to this in the bankruptcy chapter.
