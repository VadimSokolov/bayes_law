How can the outputs of legal AI be verified, explained and justified ethically? The chapter bridges formal verification and the requirements for a Bayesian machine in court. It sets out four layers of verification for language model output: existence of citations as in Mata v Avianca, logical structure, arithmetic and probability, and human judgment. It then covers retrieval augmented generation, calibration and conformal prediction, adversarial checking by a second model, and audit trails under the European Union AI Act. On explanation it compares LIME, SHAP and counterfactual explanations with a weight of evidence ledger in decibans, argues for inherently interpretable models in high stakes decisions, and discusses Article 22 of the GDPR and the AI Act right to explanation. On ethics it covers the CEPEJ European Ethical Charter, fairness as calibration and as utility, accountability, dignity and a right to a human judge, privacy of legal data, and ethics as expected utility.
