# Chapter 29. Legal Origins and Finance

This chapter presents the legal origins thesis of La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, Shleifer and Vishny (1997, 1998) that countries whose law descends from English common law protect investors more strongly than those with French, German or Scandinavian civil law, and that stronger protection goes with larger stock markets, more initial public offerings and more dispersed ownership. It explains how investor protection was measured: the antidirector rights index, creditor rights, and enforcement measures. It covers the 2008 survey on legal origin as a style of social control, the Courts paper on procedural formalism (Djankov and coauthors, 2003), and Glaeser and Shleifer's history of why origins differ. It then sets out the critics: Spamann's recoding of the index, the identification problem and colonial history (Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson; Klerman and coauthors), the great reversals of Rajan and Zingales, and Roe's politics account. A Bayesian assessment uses model averaging, measurement error models and hierarchical shrinkage across legal families, and concludes that rules matter but origin is a weaker and confounded explanation.
