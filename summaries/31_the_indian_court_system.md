This chapter describes the Indian court system and argues that delay is a decision problem. It opens with the scale of pendency reported through the National Judicial Data Grid, then covers the structure of the Supreme Court (Articles 32, 124, 136 and 142), the High Courts (Articles 226 and 227), public interest litigation (Hussainara Khatoon 1979, Article 21 and speedy trial), and Lok Adalats under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987. It reads Vivekananda's definition of law and the Nyaya idea of vyapti as a theory of induction. It discusses the Indian Evidence Act 1872 of James Fitzjames Stephen, section 3 on proof as belief or prudent action and section 114 presumptions, and the replacement statutes of 2023 (Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam). A queueing model with Little's law, the c mu rule and Gittins index for Bayesian triage, value of information, adjournments, eCourts, artificial intelligence and bail as expected utility (State of Rajasthan v Balchand, bail not jail) follow.

## From the literature

Hussainara Khatoon v Home Secretary, State of Bihar (1979); State of Rajasthan v Balchand (1977); Indian Evidence Act 1872 sections 3 and 114 (Stephen); Legal Services Authorities Act 1987; Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (2023); Vivekananda (1896) on Karma Yoga; Little's law and the c mu rule from queueing theory; the Gittins index. The queueing and triage models are the chapter's own illustrations, with stylised numbers.
