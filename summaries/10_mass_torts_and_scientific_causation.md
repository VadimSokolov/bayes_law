# Chapter 10. Mass Torts and Scientific Causation

How should courts decide scientific causation when thousands of claimants sue over a drug, device or chemical? The chapter separates general causation (can the product cause the illness, a question about the population relative risk) from specific causation (did it cause this claimant's illness). It presents Bayesian random effects meta analysis of studies on the log relative risk scale as a way to synthesise evidence and report the posterior probability that the effect is real and of a given size, and it reads the Bradford Hill considerations as reasons to update. It discusses Daubert v Merrell Dow (Bendectin) and General Electric v Joiner as gatekeeping, court appointed experts in the silicone breast implant litigation (Hall v Baxter Healthcare), probability of causation 1 minus 1 over R, differential etiology, and episodes: Agent Orange, Vioxx and glyphosate (Roundup). It closes on class actions, multidistrict litigation, bellwether trials as samples, and settlement grids that pay by probability of causation.
